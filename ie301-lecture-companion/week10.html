You tested positive. How worried should you be? Slide 12

The test is right 99% of the time, yet slide 12 gives P(sick | positive) = 9.02%. Why so low? Imagine 1,000,000 people taking the test, and follow them down the tree.

0.001
0.990
0.010

Bayes’ rule, as on slide 12: P(sick | positive) = P(positive | sick) · P(sick) / [P(positive | sick) · P(sick) + P(positive | not sick) · P(not sick)]. Multiply each term by 1,000,000 and it becomes a head count from the tree.

Cross the highway (slide 41): cars pass Poisson at 3/min; crossing takes 10 s P(no car while crossing) = —

N(t) ~ Poisson(λt) ⟺ gaps ~ exponential(λ) (slide 46). The highway answer is P(N = 0 in 10 s) = \(e^{-3\cdot 10/60} = e^{-0.5} \approx 0.61\) — and because increments are independent, it does not matter when you start crossing. The time of the n-th arrival is Erlang (slide 47).
