Produce now, roll the dice Slides 13–24

Three periods, demand each period is 1 or 2 with probability ½ each; production cost 2x + 3 (if x > 0), holding $1, end-of-period inventory ≤ 3, all demand met on time; leftovers after period 3 sell for $2. Start with 1 unit. First reveal the optimal policy, then play it against random demand and watch the average converge to the DP value.

The state after a decision is now RANDOM: producing x from inventory i leads to i + x − 1 or i + x − 2, each with probability ½ — so the recursion averages the two futures (slide 20). An optimal solution is no longer a fixed plan but a policy: at stage 1 produce 3, then react to whatever demand materializes (slide 24).

Sell the laptop: when do you stop? Extra · stopping rules

You are selling an old laptop and can collect bids for at most 3 days. Each day exactly one offer arrives: 1500 TL with probability 0.30, 2000 TL with 0.50, or 2500 TL with 0.20. Accept and the sale is done; reject and that offer is gone for good. On day 3 you must take whatever comes. Play 10 independent rounds — highest total in class wins. Everyone in the same class session gets the same offers, so this compares decisions, not luck.

Round 1, day 1 — accept this offer, or gamble on tomorrow?

Work backwards. Let \( f_n \) be the expected earnings from day \( n \) onward when you play well.

day n\( f_n^* \)accept an offer if …
31950always (nothing follows)

\( f_3 = 0.3(1500)+0.5(2000)+0.2(2500) = 1950 \). On day 2 you compare each offer with 1950, which gives \( f_2 = 0.3(1950)+0.5(2000)+0.2(2500) = 2085 \). On day 1 you compare with 2085, so only 2500 is worth taking, and \( f_1 = 0.8(2085)+0.2(2500) = 2168 \).

The rule is a threshold that drops as time runs out: picky on day 1, less picky on day 2, forced on day 3. Holding out early is worth about 11% more per round than grabbing whatever arrives first (2168 vs 1950).

Ten rounds is a small sample — roughly one class in twelve, the take-day-1 player gets lucky and finishes ahead. That does not make the rule wrong: it maximises the average, not every single round. Watch the averages settle:
