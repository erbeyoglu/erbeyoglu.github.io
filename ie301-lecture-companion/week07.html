Beat me if you can: the match puzzle Slides 4–7

  

    30 matches. Two sides take turns; each turn takes 1, 2 or 3 — whoever takes the last match loses.
    In class the instructor plays against the students: the instructor
    moves first, then the class agrees on its move and the instructor clicks it. The buttons take the colour of
    the side whose turn it is. At home, tick Simulate instructor: the instructor's moves are then made
    for you. Can the students win?

    spoiler — open after the class has answeredWorking backwards (slides 6–7): facing 1 match you lose; facing 2–4 you win; facing 5 you lose…
    the losing counts are 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29 — every 4k+1. From 30, taking 1 leaves 29 and you
    can then mirror every opponent move to keep leaving 4k+1. That backward sweep IS dynamic programming.

    Slide 22’s numbers appear at m = 5, k = 5: 3125 paths → 15 625 additions by enumeration, but only
    (m − 1)k² + k = 105 by DP. Enumeration grows exponentially (kᵐ); DP grows linearly in the number of
