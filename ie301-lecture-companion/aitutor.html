What this is

The hardest skill in this course is formulating the model — choosing stages, states, decisions and the recursion for a DP; variables, objective and constraints for an NLP; states and transitions for a Markov chain. This prompt asks your chatbot to act as a Socratic tutor that works through your problem with you — one modeling task at a time, adapting help when you are stuck — and is instructed to build on your attempts before recapping the model. It runs in the AI account you already have (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, …); nothing to install, no separate account needed.

Each model family has its own six-part structure:

Dynamic programming

1 · Understanding2 · Stages 3 · State4 · Decisions 5 · Transition & recursion6 · Boundary & answer

Your DP card also tracks the feasible decision set, the meaning of the value function, and the transition as separate entries.

Nonlinear programming

1 · Understanding2 · Variables 3 · Objective4 · Constraints 5 · Classify & check6 · Solution path & answer

KKT belongs to the solution path, when appropriate for the model and variable domains.

Markov chains

1 · Understanding2 · States 3 · Dynamics4 · Classification 5 · Toolbox match6 · Answer

Your earlier attempts count. If a hint is not enough, the tutor can explain one component and ask you to apply it, or practise an easier prerequisite with you before returning. Once your formulation is checked, you can request arithmetic, tables or code; you still choose the method and interpret the result. Missing data and assumptions should stay explicit.

Start a session

  1. Click Copy tutor prompt — the tutor's full instructions land on your clipboard.
  2. Open a new chat in your AI app and paste. The tutor greets you and asks for your problem.
  3. Paste your problem — or send a photo of the textbook page — in the chat, and start.
The button only copies text to your clipboard — there is no server and no account behind this tool.

Please read

  • AI makes mistakes — sometimes confidently. It can slip on arithmetic, misread numbers or handwriting in a photo, and state a wrong step with full confidence. Double-check the numbers yourself, and treat the tutor as a study partner, not an authority. The authoritative sources for this course are the lecture slides, the recitations, and your instructor — if the tutor and the slides disagree, the slides win; bring anything you are unsure about to class or office hours.

Before you copy — please read

    Getting the most out of it

    Use a fresh chat for each problem. Old conversations water the instructions down.
    Photos work. Inside the chat you can send a photo of the textbook page or of your handwritten attempt — the tutor reads it back to you and checks it got the numbers right.
    Show your attempt. Even an unfinished state definition helps the tutor find the gap. If you cannot begin, ask for a smaller first step.
    English is recommended — the course, the exams and the terminology are in English — but if you prefer Turkish, say so and the tutor will switch.
    If the chatbot starts solving instead of asking, remind it: "you are my Socratic tutor — help me formulate one component at a time." If it makes a mistake, challenge it and ask it to recheck the affected parts of your model card. Long chats sometimes drift.
