spoiler — open after the class has answeredThe unconstrained peak (15, 11.25) is outside the region, so the optimum slides down to the boundary: (12, 9) with f = 270, where ∇f = (3, 6) = 3·(1, 2) — parallel to the constraint normal, with multiplier u = 3. With the linear objective the level lines are straight and the best point is the corner (30, 0). Spot Q, c, A and b Slides 4–7, 23

Write the problem in the slide-4 form: max f(x) = cᵀx − ½ xᵀQx subject to Ax ≤ b, x ≥ 0 (with Q symmetric). Fill in the entries and check — the −½ and the cross-term split are where everyone slips.

spoiler — open after the class has answeredSlide 7’s recipe: qⱼⱼ = −2 × (coefficient of xⱼ²) and qᵢⱼ = qⱼᵢ = −(coefficient of xᵢxⱼ), because the ½ halves the diagonal and the symmetric pair qᵢⱼ + qⱼᵢ shares the cross term. A and b come straight from the ≤ constraints: one row of A per constraint, its right-hand side in b; x ≥ 0 stays separate and is not a row of A. Q positive semidefinite ⇔ f concave ⇔ KKT is necessary AND sufficient (slide 9).
